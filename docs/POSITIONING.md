# QuietPatch positioning

## What the current board already covers

The live submission board already includes projects focused on hiring, identity, eligibility, health, education, credit, solvency, payroll, insurance, and energy infrastructure. QuietPatch should stay out of those categories.

## The gap

Security disclosure has a different privacy failure. The sensitive object is the vulnerability report itself. Publishing it too early can harm users; hiding it completely makes trust and payment harder.

## Product wedge

QuietPatch starts with open-source maintainers who need a simple private intake and public process record. The first version does not compete on researcher rankings, automated scanning, or a large marketplace. It owns the handoff between encrypted evidence and a maintainer decision.

## What makes the project distinct

- The private object is evidence, not a personal credential.
- The public output is a process record, not an eligibility badge.
- The maintainer remains part of the decision; the contract does not pretend that a score proves a bug.
- The demo shows a real privacy boundary in one screen: the maintainer can read the report while the observer cannot.

## Later products

After the first release, the same flow can support:

- private reports for Midnight dApps;
- coordinated disclosure between several maintainers;
- a reviewer escrow or appeal process;
- privacy-preserving researcher reputation;
- payout adapters for supported assets.

Those are follow-on releases. They should not enter the Wave 1 contract unless the main flow already works.
